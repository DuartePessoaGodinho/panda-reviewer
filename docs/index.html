Runs from your cloned repo against the MR source and target branches, then saves review history locally for the next pass.

Tray signalQuiet when clear. Orange when your queue needs attention.
What it showcases

A small desktop app for the parts of code review people actually repeat.

Review tools usually sell dashboards. Panda Reviewer sells fewer interruptions: a queue you can trust, local project context, and the fastest path from "someone needs me" to "review done".

MR queue, not a browser tab

Separate "To Review" and "My MRs" views keep incoming review requests and your own waiting work visible without opening GitLab.

Diffs, comments, CI state

Cards expose draft, approval, CI, comment, branch, project, and freshness signals before you decide where to spend attention.

Open the local repo

Configure repo folders once, then jump straight into VS Code, IntelliJ, or your preferred editor when the MR needs deeper work.

AI review as a second pass

Run a local CLI review with your provider of choice, add context, cancel if needed, and keep the generated review history per MR.

Local and transparent

The app talks to your GitLab instance with your token. There is no Panda Reviewer account, hosted backend, analytics, or sync layer.

Built for self-managed GitLab

Point it at GitLab.com or an internal GitLab URL, then use a personal access token with API scope to fetch the data you already have access to.

Workflow

The Panda is there when review work appears, then gets out of the way.

The page now mirrors the real app behavior: polling, queue triage, local repo detection, optional AI review, and quick approval. That is the story users of this kind of app expect to understand fast.

Configure GitLab once

Add your GitLab URL, paste a token with API scope, choose repo folders, and pick your IDE.

Let the tray watch the queue

Panda Reviewer polls in the background and changes from calm to attention-needed when MRs move.

Review with context

Open a diff, jump into the local branch, run an AI review, or approve directly from the desktop app.

Keep history close

AI review output is saved locally per MR so follow-up passes do not start from a blank page.

Small mascot, serious job.

The Panda gives the page a memory hook, but the product still reads as a practical developer tool: fast queue triage, fewer stale reviews, and local control.

For reviewers and authors

Review work has two sides. Panda Reviewer shows both.

Most MR tools focus on one inbox. This app also keeps your own open MRs in front of you, so you can follow up when CI fails, someone leaves comments, or an approval arrives.

As a reviewer

Prioritize by freshness, CI status, comments, approvals, and draft state. Open the diff only when the card says it is worth your time.

As an author

Watch your own MRs without refreshing GitLab. See when feedback lands and avoid leaving fixes or approvals idle.
